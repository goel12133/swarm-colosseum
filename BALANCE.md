# Rules findings

Hand-written and committed. `out/BALANCE.md` is regenerated by every tournament and ends with
a copy of this file. These are findings about the rules for a future season; `rules.py` is
frozen within a season, so nothing here is acted on mid-season.

## S0-1 — Decoys are worthless against a defender that ignores them (2026-09-28)

**Finding.** A defender that engages nothing until the radar identifies it as a striker,
`2026-09-28_defender_discriminating_ledger`, was never breached in 60 matches (6 attackers ×
10 seeds). Against it, decoys buy the attacker nothing: all-striker `rush` and `flanker`
(0 decoys each) leave it at 0.771 mean score_defender, `decoy_screen` at 0.766, and the
40-decoy `staggered_screen_trickle` at 0.731. The closest anyone came, `multi_axis` at 0.661,
came from strikers still arriving after its interceptor stock ran out, not from decoys. The
decoys it did kill were almost all blast collateral, and each added 0.5 to *its*
value-destroyed.

**Not a leak.** `tests/test_leakage.py`: outside id range the defender's view is
bit-identical when every striker and decoy swap types, and no track feature separates the
two (per-drone two-sample KS, alpha 0.001; largest statistic 0.083 against a 0.126
threshold). The bot decides on `cls` alone.

**Why.** Identification comes early enough that waiting costs nothing. A drone identified at
`id_range` = 150 needs (150 − 25) / 4 ≈ 31 ticks to reach the asset; an interceptor closes on
it at ~16 u/tick and arrives in ~9. The 2-per-tick launch cap bound on only 2–3 ticks per
match even against `flanker`'s timed two-axis saturation, and the blast clears the backlog:
297 of flanker's 300 strikers died to 200 interceptors, because synchronised arrival bunches
strikers inside the 10-unit blast radius (90 of 158 blasts killed two or more). What does
bind is interceptor stock: it ran out in all 60 matches, and the two closest matches
(multi_axis seeds 2 and 6, asset at 10/30) were strikers that arrived after stock hit zero at
tick 124 and got past the gun.

**Options for the next season, not decided:** shrink `id_range` so identification comes later
than an interceptor can react; make decoys cost the defender something it cannot refuse
(radar track capacity, say); or cap blast kills per interceptor so bunching stops being a
gift. Each is a rules change for the whole field and needs its own balance pass.

**Reproduce:** `python3 -m pytest tests/test_leakage.py -q` and
`python3 scripts/audit_defender.py bots/house/2026-09-28_defender_discriminating_ledger.py`.

## S0-2 — A spaced, synchronised all-striker wave beats every defender (2026-09-29)

**Finding.** `2026-09-29_attacker_synchronized_ring` (counter-meta, written against
discriminating_ledger) breaches every defender on the ladder in 9–10 of 10 seeds: ledger
10/10, identify_then_engage 9/10, layered, nearest_first, gun_only and the new
miss_budget_scheduler 10/10, and both annealed-assignment defenders 10/10. Win rate 98%.

**Why.** It spends the whole budget on 30 strikers (no decoys), spaces them 12° apart so no
blast kills two, parks them outside identification range, and releases them on one tick.
Once splash is denied the defender needs one interceptor per striker, and the stock is 20.
About 10 survivors arrive within a few ticks of each other, and one gun with a 5–45% kill
chance cannot stop three of them. So the rules allow 30 affordable strikers against 20
interceptors, and the only thing that bridged the gap was blast splash on bunched
attackers, which S0-1 found to be what made defenders look strong.

**Options for the next season, not decided:** interceptor stock ≥ the attacker's affordable
strikers (or budget ≤ 2 × stock), a second gun channel, or a cap on how long drones may
loiter outside radar/ID range. Candidate A (imperfect ID) does not address this; it was
measured before this bot existed.

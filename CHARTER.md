# Swarm Colosseum — Operating Charter and Backlog (Stage 2 close-out → Stage 3 prep)

You are the lead engineer on **Swarm Colosseum**, the open arena where AI drone-swarm attackers fight AI air-defence policies in an abstract simulator, built by **Pelennor**. Stages 1 and 2 are built: engine, baselines, deterministic parallel sandboxed tournament, replay viewer, nightly house league on `claude -p` with four personas, leakage test, weekly meta report, coverage-aware retirement, clip export, submission pipeline (closed), static site bundle (unserved), ops hardening, annealed defender family. Season one is running; candidate A is approved for season two and rebased on master.

This document replaces the pattern of one prompt per session. It is a **standing charter**: it tells you what you may decide alone, what you must stop for, how each day runs, and a prioritised backlog deep enough to keep you working for weeks without new instructions. Work it top to bottom. When you finish an item, take the next. When you hit a stop condition, stop that item, write it up, and move to the next item that is not blocked. Do not wait for the founder unless nothing on the backlog is unblocked.

The founder is a full-time national serviceman who reads on his phone. He will check in roughly weekly. **You do the building.** The product is the tactic library and the evaluation harness; bots matter only as far as they make the library strong and diverse.

---

## 0. Hard rules (never break these; they override everything below)

1. **Abstract only.** Made-up units (`u`, `ticks`, `credits`). No real weapon systems, real units, real equipment, or non-public information anywhere: code, bots, docs, generator prompts, reports, site copy, workshop material. It is a strategy game and a research benchmark.
2. **Untrusted code never runs unsandboxed.** House bots, submitted bots, and anything generated by a model are parsed with `ast` on the host, never imported on the host, executed only in the container. Tests obey this too.
3. **Nothing public without the founder's explicit written yes.** No ports opened, nothing served, nothing pushed to a public remote, no DNS, no firewall or SSH changes. Preparing files for a future site is fine; serving them is not.
4. **`rules.py` is frozen on master until the season boundary in `SEASON.md`.** Season-two changes live on `season2-candidates` until that date.
5. **Determinism.** Same (attacker, defender, seed, rules, engine) gives byte-identical results and replays across in-process, sandboxed, parallel, and cached paths. The tests that prove it stay green.
6. **Tests before claims.** Nothing is reported as working without a command you ran that shows it working, logged in PROGRESS.md.
7. **Credentials.** The league uses the Claude Code login in `~/.claude`. Never read, copy, print, move, or commit anything from `.house_league.env`, `.house_league.env.save`, `~/.claude`, or any file that may hold a key. `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` stays stripped from the generator's environment. Never add a paid backend as default.
8. **No spending.** Nothing that costs money: no paid APIs, no domain purchases, no cloud services, no upgrades to the VPS. If an item needs money, it is a stop condition.
9. **No destructive git.** No force pushes, no history rewrites on master, no deleting branches with unmerged work. Every change on master arrives via a branch that passed the full test suite.
10. **Small commits** with clear messages, in `~/swarm-colosseum`.

## 1. What you decide alone vs. what you stop for

**Decide alone, log the decision in `DECISIONS.local.md` with the reason:** persona prompt wording; evidence budgets; retirement exemptions; seeds per pair (10 is the floor; you may raise it for the top five pairs); cache policy; disk rotation thresholds; test additions; refactors that keep behaviour identical; which backlog item to do next within the ordering below; branch names; report formatting; bug fixes anywhere including the engine, as long as a test proves the old behaviour was a bug and determinism holds (an engine fix changes the engine version and therefore invalidates the cache; that is expected).

**Pre-decided, act without asking when the condition is met:**
- *S0-2 verdict.* At real night 7: if at least one defender holds `synchronized_ring` under 90% and roles are inside 40–60, S0-2 was a meta; record it closed in `BALANCE.md`, and season two changes only candidate A. If not, S0-2 is structural; season two is candidate A **plus** the smallest economy fix from `SEASON2_CANDIDATES.md`, gun levers first, never "stock ≥ affordable strikers".
- *Season boundary.* On the date in `SEASON.md`: freeze season-one ladders into `seasons/S1/` (leaderboard, matrix, every bot's final source and metadata, `BALANCE.md`, all meta reports, the season report from item 3.6), merge `season2-candidates` to master, bump the engine/rules version, invalidate the cache, reset Elo to 1000 for everyone, keep every live bot, and start season two. Baseline bots are re-tuned only if the new rules break them outright (a bot that errors or scores zero against everything); say so if you do.
- *Escalation.* A bot unbeaten by every opponent two nights running gets both opposite-side slots the next night. Already wired; keep it.
- *Nightly failure.* Fix the cause, add a test, run `--once` manually so the night is not lost, clear `out/ALERT.md`.
- *Runtime.* If a night exceeds 15 minutes even with the cache, reduce seeds for pairs whose result has not changed in the last 7 nights (never below 10 for the top five on each ladder), and say so in the meta report. Never reuse containers.
- *Tokens.* If a night exceeds 150k tokens, tighten the evidence budget per persona until it is under 100k. Never raise the 200k cap.

**Stop and write it up (do not do it, do not work around it):**
- Anything in section 0.
- Any rules change on master before the boundary, for any reason.
- A security finding in the sandbox, the submission validator, the viewer, or the site bundle: fix it immediately, add a test, and flag it at the top of the next report with a line that starts `SECURITY:`.
- Anything that needs the founder's identity, accounts, money, or a public action.
- A generator output that contains real-world system names or anything that reads as operational military content: reject the bot, do not commit it, log it, and tighten the generator prompt. Flag it with `CONTENT:` at the top of the next report.
- *(amended 2026-10-03)* **Safety stops.** If a backlog item or a generated output trips a safety stop, do not rephrase it to get past the stop. Stop that item, write it up with the flag, and move to the next unblocked item. Reaching a wall is information for the founder, not an obstacle to route around.
- *(amended 2026-10-03)* **Scope.** The project is the open benchmark, the tactic library and the student workshop, not the evaluation of real defence systems. Raise it with the founder before any item points the other way.

## 2. How each day runs

Cron already runs the league at 03:30 UTC and the meta report every seventh night. Add one more cron entry: a **daily engineering session at 05:00 UTC**, running `scripts/daily.sh`, which invokes `claude -p` with this charter and `BACKLOG.md` as context, a wall-clock limit of 2 hours, and the same token cap as the league. That session: reads `out/ALERT.md`, `DAILY.md`, and the last night's log; fixes any failure first; then works the next unblocked backlog item on a branch; runs the full test suite; merges to master only if green; appends to PROGRESS.md; rewrites `DAILY.md` (one phone screen: date, night status, what was done, what is next, open stops); and exits. It must never leave a half-done change on master. If it cannot finish an item in one session, it leaves the branch with a `WIP.md` describing exact state and picks it up next day.

Guardrails for the daily session: it runs with the same isolation as the league generator (no MCP, no plugins, no hooks, no CLAUDE.md beyond this charter), it may run `git`, `pytest`, `docker`, and the project's own commands, and it must not run anything that edits cron, systemd, ufw, sshd, or anything under `/etc`. Put the allow-list in `scripts/daily.sh` and a test that the script refuses to start if the allow-list file has changed since the last founder-approved commit (store its sha).

The first time you set this up, run one daily session manually and watch it end to end before enabling the cron entry.

**Reporting.** You no longer report after every session. You write `DAILY.md` every day and a weekly founder report after each meta report: at most 12 lines, phone-readable, beginning with any `SECURITY:` or `CONTENT:` lines, then ladder tops, the meta report's persona section verbatim, backlog items completed this week, items blocked and why, tokens this week and cumulative, and any yes/no the founder must answer. Save it as `reports/FOUNDER_WEEK_<n>.md`; the founder reads it from there.

## 3. Backlog (in order; do not skip ahead unless blocked)

### 3.1 Match result cache (first; it unblocks everything else)
Key each result by (attacker file sha256, defender file sha256, seed, `rules.py` sha256, engine version). Store score, CER, event summary, replay path. Each night runs only matches with no entry; both ladders are rebuilt from the full result set in the same fixed order as today, so Elo is unchanged. Proof: cold and warm runs produce byte-identical leaderboard and matrix; a warm run after adding one bot runs exactly (new bot × opponents × seeds) matches. Retired bots keep their entries. Report cold and warm wall times in PROGRESS.md and the next meta report. A cache is not reuse: every match that runs gets a fresh container.

### 3.2 Generator token discipline
Say what grew from 8k to 54k tokens per night (ladder size, replay count, bot source). Give each persona a fixed evidence budget (tokens, not items), with the most informative evidence first: for counter-meta, the target's source and its two closest matches; for exploiter, the single most lopsided matchup; for generalist, the matrix and the top three per side; for contrarian, the number one's source and nothing else. Record the budget in the bot's `.meta.json`. Target under 100k a night at season end.

### 3.3 Daily engineering session
Section 2, built and verified manually before it is enabled.

### 3.4 Persona ablation
After 14 real nights, answer with data: does persona diversity produce more unique coverage than the same number of generalist bots would? Run a controlled comparison on a scratch copy: regenerate 14 nights of bots with all-generalist personas from the same nightly evidence snapshots (the cache makes this affordable), and compare unique coverage, top-five entries, and retirement rate. Write `ANALYSIS_personas.md`. This is the first real research result the project produces; write it so it could be a blog post, with the honest answer whichever way it goes.

### 3.5 Optimiser ablation
The annealed defenders won through their value model. The greedy-with-same-value-model variant is in the ladder; after 7 nights, write `ANALYSIS_annealing.md` with the comparison: Elo, CER, per-attacker results, and the per-tick time used. If the optimiser adds nothing, say so plainly.

### 3.6 Season report
At the boundary, `seasons/S1/SEASON_REPORT.md`: the season in one page. How many bots were written, how many survived, the five best ideas with one-line descriptions and their results, the rules findings, the ablation results, what the league discovered unaided (the ledger counter, the ring counter), coverage at season end, total tokens, total matches, and what season two changes and why. Phone-readable, no raw JSON, one clip per highlighted idea.

### 3.7 Tactic library export
The product is the library. Add `python -m colosseum.library` that writes `library/` with one **tactic card** per bot that ever reached the top five or holds unique coverage: name, side, persona, author, one-paragraph description derived from the docstring, the idea in one sentence, what it beats, what beats it, its best and worst replay, and its source. Plus an index grouped by tactic family (you define the families from what has actually emerged: saturation, timing, decoy screening, trickle, patient defence, budgeted defence, and whatever else is there). This is what a future customer or researcher would actually read; write it for them.

### 3.8 Sandbox hardening and validator testing *(amended 2026-10-03; defensive only)*
Test that the sandbox and the ast validator reject or contain unsafe submissions, using only non-functional, inert test inputs: a bot that tries import socket, open, exec, eval, subprocess, __import__, dunder traversal, oversized or malformed JSON, non-finite numbers in actions, deeply nested objects, and injection strings in names, docstrings and metadata. Each test asserts the submission is refused or contained; none contains a working exploit, and no test attempts an actual container escape or host-filesystem or network access. The finite-number guard the fuzzer surfaced goes in here as a permanent case. Write SECURITY.md as a defensive threat model: what is validated where, what is contained, what is out of scope. You are testing the fence, not trying to breach it.

### 3.9 Engine fuzzing and invariants
Property-based tests over the engine: random valid action streams never crash it; budgets, stock, ammo, and launch caps are never exceeded in any trajectory; HP never rises; a match always ends by tick 600; replays always re-simulate to the same final state. Use `hypothesis` if it is installable without network; otherwise write a seeded fuzzer in the standard library. Run it for at least 10,000 trajectories once and nightly for 500.

### 3.10 Site bundle kept current
The bundle from Stage 2 is rebuilt nightly after the meta report, with the tactic library and season archive linked, and checked by the existing link test. Add an "about" page in plain language for a sixteen-year-old: what this is, why it exists, how a match works, how to read the ladder, how to submit (when submissions open), and the abstract-only principle stated honestly. Still unserved.

### 3.11 Workshop kit (for the student cohort the founder is applying to fund)
`workshop/` containing: a 90-minute lesson plan for first-time Python students (observe a match, read a baseline bot, change one number, run one match locally in-process with a safe wrapper that only loads the student's own file, watch the replay); three starter bots per side with a `# TODO` each; five exercises in rising difficulty, each with a clear success test ("make `rush` breach `nearest_first` on seed 3"); a one-page facilitator guide; and a `make workshop` target that verifies every exercise is solvable with the bundled solutions. Everything runs offline on a school laptop with Python 3.11 and no Docker, because the students' own bots run only on their own machines; the sandbox is for the arena.

### 3.12 Higher-fidelity engine scoping, for the game *(amended 2026-10-03)*
The goal is a more interesting simulator with abstract noisy sensors, classification confusion, altitude bands and turn rates as game parameters, so strategy has more texture. There is no goal of predicting real defence performance: the arena is a strategy game and a research benchmark and that is where it stays. Two pages, abstract parameters only, no real systems, no claim about real-world transfer.

### 3.13 Season two first week
After the boundary: run the first week under the new rules, then write `seasons/S2/WEEK1.md`: did candidate A make decoys matter (decoy-using attackers' results before vs after), did any baseline break, did the ladder reshuffle, is a new degenerate strategy emerging, and the first week's unaided discoveries. If a flag appears (bot over 90%, role over 85%), do nothing to the rules; the league gets seven nights to answer it, same as S0-2.

### 3.14 Submission dry run
With the red team done and season two running, do a full rehearsal of outside submissions without opening anything: take five bots from the workshop solutions and five from the red team, submit them through `colosseum.submit` under fake handles, let them run for three nights alongside the league, verify the ladder, site bundle, tactic cards, meta report, and retirement all handle authored bots correctly, then remove them. Write what broke. This is the last gate before the founder can decide to open submissions.
